\section{The idea}
THz access points (APs) need line of sight, and the people they serve are also what blocks
them. TeraMove fixed blockage by \emph{moving} APs. TeraMove-H tries two cheaper fixes first:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a \textbf{Wi-Fi layer} together with smarter \textbf{user association};
\item \textbf{power control}: idle APs are switched off, and busy APs use less power.
\end{enumerate}
APs move only if these electronic fixes are not enough.

\section{Link model}
\subsection{THz path loss (dB)}
\begin{keybox}[Path loss]
\[
L_{a,u}=\underbrace{20\log_{10}\frac{4\pi f d}{c}}_{\text{free space}}
+\underbrace{4.343\,k(f)\,d}_{\text{absorption}}
+\underbrace{L^{\mathrm{st}}_{a,u}}_{\text{walls}}
+\underbrace{\min\!\big(n_{a,u}L_h,\;L_h^{\max}\big)}_{\text{bodies}}
+\underbrace{L_{\mathrm{sb}}\,\mathbf{1}^{\mathrm{sb}}_{a,u}}_{\text{own body}}
\]
\end{keybox}
\why{Each term is a separate reason the signal gets weaker, so the losses (in dB) simply add up. \emph{Free space}: the wave spreads over a growing sphere, so power falls with $d^2$; at 300\,GHz this is already about 82\,dB at 1\,m. \emph{Absorption}: water vapour in the air soaks up THz energy, a fixed number of dB per metre. \emph{Walls}: each wall crossed costs a fixed amount that depends on its material. \emph{Bodies}: a person in the path costs about 20\,dB; we cap the total at 40\,dB because after two bodies the link is dead anyway. \emph{Own body}: if the AP is behind you, your own body blocks it.}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l p{0.46\textwidth} l@{}}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning & Value\\
\midrule
$f$ & carrier frequency & 300\,GHz\\
$d$ & 3-D AP--user distance & --\\
$c$ & speed of light & --\\
$k(f)$ & molecular absorption (ITU-R P.676) & $1.54\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ (6.68\,dB/km)\\
$L^{\mathrm{st}}$ & wall penalty per wall crossed & concrete 30, wood 20, glass 15\,dB\\
$n_{a,u}$ & bodies whose circle the ray crosses at height $\le1.6$\,m & --\\
$L_h,\ L_h^{\max}$ & body loss, cap & 20\,dB, 40\,dB\\
$\mathbf{1}^{\mathrm{sb}}$ & AP lies behind the user's own body (3GPP self-blocking region) & 0 or 1\\
$L_{\mathrm{sb}}$ & self-blocking loss & 30\,dB\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Linear path gain:
\[
\ell_{a,u}=10^{-L_{a,u}/10}.
\]
\why{Powers from different APs must be \emph{added}, and adding only works on linear values, not on dB.}

\subsection{AP antenna gain (energy conserving)}
The main lobe covers the lower half-space within $\pm\alpha/2$ of $\phi_a$. Its solid angle is
$\alpha$ steradians, so
\begin{keybox}[AP gain]
\[
G_m(\alpha)=\frac{4\pi-g_s\,(4\pi-\alpha)}{\alpha},
\qquad g_s=-10\ \text{dBi (side lobe)},\quad \alpha\ \text{in radians}.
\]
\end{keybox}
\why{An antenna cannot create energy; it only redirects it. Averaged over all directions the gain must be 1 (i.e.\ $4\pi$ when integrated), so $G_m\cdot\alpha + g_s(4\pi-\alpha)=4\pi$. Solving for $G_m$ gives the formula: squeeze the beam (small $\alpha$) and the main gain goes up.}
A wider beam gives less gain: \textbf{13.4 / 10.4 / 7.4\,dBi} for $\alpha$ = 30$^\circ$ / 60$^\circ$ / 120$^\circ$.
The AP gain towards user $u$ is
\[
G^{t}_{a,u}=
\begin{cases}
G_m(\alpha_a), & \Delta\phi_{a,u}\le\alpha_a/2,\\[2pt]
g_s, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
\why{A user inside the sector gets the full beam; outside it, only the weak side-lobe leakage.}

\subsection{User device antenna}
The device points a $G_r=20$\,dBi pencil beam at its serving AP. The same energy rule fixes
the main-lobe cone, with half-angle $\psi$:
\[
\Omega_r=\frac{4\pi(1-g_r)}{G_r-g_r},
\qquad
\psi=\arccos\!\Big(1-\frac{\Omega_r}{2\pi}\Big)=10.9^\circ .
\]
\why{Same energy rule for the phone's pencil beam. A cone of half-angle $\psi$ covers $2\pi(1-\cos\psi)$ steradians, which gives $\psi$. Only APs inside this narrow cone can interfere strongly with the user.}
$G^{r}_{u,a,b}$ is the device gain towards AP $b$ when the beam points at AP $a$. It equals
$G_r$ if $b$ lies within $\psi$ of the direction to $a$, and $g_r=-10$\,dBi otherwise.

\subsection{SINR and capacity}
Write $h_{b,u}=G^{t}_{b,u}\,\ell_{b,u}$ for the channel gain.
\begin{keybox}[{SINR, noise and capacity}]
\[
\mathrm{SINR}_{u,a}=\frac{p_a\,h_{a,u}\,G_r}{N_0+\displaystyle\sum_{b\ne a}p_b\,h_{b,u}\,G^{r}_{u,a,b}}
\]
\[
N_0=-174+10\log_{10}B+\mathrm{NF}=-174+100+10=-64\ \text{dBm}
\]
\[
c_{u,a}=
\begin{cases}
B\,\min\!\big(\log_2(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{u,a}),\,6\big), & \mathrm{SINR}_{u,a}\ge\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}=5\ \text{dB},\\[2pt]
0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad B=10\ \text{GHz}.
\]
\end{keybox}
\why{\emph{SINR}: useful power divided by everything unwanted (noise plus the power of all other APs at this user). \emph{Noise}: thermal noise is $-174$\,dBm per Hz, so a wide 10\,GHz channel collects $+100$\,dB of it, plus 10\,dB added by the receiver. \emph{Capacity}: Shannon's formula gives bits/s/Hz $=\log_2(1+\mathrm{SINR})$; we cap it at 6 (64-QAM, the best practical modulation) and set it to 0 below 5\,dB, where a THz link cannot be held.}

\subsection{Wi-Fi link}
3GPP InH-Office path loss, with $f_c=6$\,GHz:
\[
\mathrm{PL}_{\mathrm{LOS}}=32.4+17.3\log_{10}d+20\log_{10}f_c,
\]
\[
\mathrm{PL}_{\mathrm{NLOS}}=\max\!\big(\mathrm{PL}_{\mathrm{LOS}},\;17.3+38.3\log_{10}d+24.9\log_{10}f_c\big).
\]
\why{These are standard 3GPP curves fitted to indoor measurements. The distance exponent is 1.73 in line of sight and 3.83 when walls or people are in the way. The $\max$ makes sure a blocked link is never better than a clear one.}
A link is LOS only if no full-height wall and no body is in the way. The rate is the
2-stream 160\,MHz PHY rate of the highest 802.11ax MCS whose sensitivity is met, times a MAC
efficiency of 0.7. The maximum Wi-Fi rate is about \textbf{1.68\,Gb/s}.

\section{User association (the core)}
\subsection{Airtime}
Every AP time-shares its channel. If user $u$ is served by AP $a$, it needs this share of
the AP's time:
\begin{keybox}[Airtime needed]
\[
\tau_{u,a}=\frac{\Rmin}{c_{u,a}}
\]
\end{keybox}
\why{To deliver $\Rmin$ over a link of speed $c$, the AP must spend a fraction $\Rmin/c$ of its time on that user. Example: 1\,Gb/s on a 40\,Gb/s link needs 2.5\,\% of the time.}
Each AP can give out at most \textbf{1} unit of time in total.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Link & Capacity & Airtime needed at 1\,Gb/s\\
\midrule
Good THz & 40\,Gb/s & 0.025\\
Good Wi-Fi & 1.5\,Gb/s & 0.67 (two-thirds of the whole AP)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Optimisation problem}
Let $x_{u,a}=1$ if user $u$ is served by AP $a$, and 0 otherwise.
\begin{keybox}[Association problem (maximum GAP)]
\begin{align*}
\max_{\mathbf{x}}\quad & \sum_{u}\sum_{a}x_{u,a}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad & \sum_{a}x_{u,a}\le1 && \forall u \quad\text{(at most one AP per user)}\\
& \sum_{u}\tau_{u,a}\,x_{u,a}\le1 && \forall a \quad\text{(airtime budget)}\\
& x_{u,a}=0\ \text{ if } c_{u,a}<\Rmin && \text{(link too weak)}\\
& x_{u,a}\in\{0,1\}
\end{align*}
\end{keybox}
\why{We count served users. Each user takes one AP at most; an AP cannot hand out more than 100\,\% of its time; and a link slower than $\Rmin$ is useless even with all the time.}
\textbf{Satisfaction} is the fraction of users who get at least $\Rmin$:
\[
S=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{u}\sum_{a}x_{u,a}.
\]
\why{Satisfaction is simply the share of users who got what they asked for.}
This is a \textbf{maximum Generalised Assignment Problem (GAP)} in which every user has the
same value. Think of packing items (users) into bins (APs), where an item's size depends on
the bin it goes into.

\subsection{Why it is hard (Proposition 1)}
Deciding whether \emph{all} users can be served is NP-complete. It contains the PARTITION
problem: given integers $s_1,\dots,s_k$ with $\sum_i s_i=2T$, can they be split into two halves
of equal sum? Use two APs and choose the capacities so that
\[
c_{u,1}=c_{u,2}=\frac{\Rmin\,T}{s_u}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tau_{u,a}=\frac{s_u}{T}.
\]
Both airtime budgets hold with everyone served \textbf{if and only if} the integers split into
two halves that each sum to $T$. So an exact solver is too slow for every evaluation, and we
use a greedy algorithm.
\why{If an exact solver could always answer quickly, it would also solve PARTITION quickly, which is believed impossible. So we need a fast heuristic.}

\subsection{Greedy algorithm (\texttt{gap\_greedy})}
\begin{enumerate}
\item List every usable (user, AP) pair, THz and Wi-Fi together.
\item Sort the pairs by airtime $\tau_{u,a}$, smallest first.
\item Walk down the list. Assign a pair only if the user is still free \textbf{and} the AP
  still has enough time left.
\item Users left over are unserved.
\end{enumerate}
The cost is $O(KM\log KM)$ for $K$ users and $M$ APs. Cheapest pairs first leaves room for
the most users. With a single AP this rule is exactly optimal.
\why{Short tasks first: giving time to the users who need the least of it leaves the most time for others, like packing small items into a suitcase first. Because THz and Wi-Fi pairs are in one list, a blocked user automatically falls back to Wi-Fi, and a full THz AP pushes users to a second-best AP.}

\subsection{When association matters}
Gain of the greedy GAP over threshold offload, in satisfaction:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccccc@{}}
\toprule
$\Rmin$ (Gb/s) & 0.1 & 0.25 & 0.5 & 1 & 2\\
\midrule
GAP $-$ offload (pp) & +1.5 & +1.9 & +1.3 & +0.8 & 0.0\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low demand.} Each Wi-Fi AP can carry many users, so sharing its time wisely
  pays off. At 0.1\,Gb/s, re-association alone serves \textbf{93.2\,\%} of users, and
  \textbf{78\,\%} of crowd snapshots need no AP movement at all.
\item \textbf{High demand.} One Wi-Fi AP tops out at 1.68\,Gb/s, so it carries about one user
  at 1\,Gb/s. There is little left to allocate, and the rule barely matters.
\end{itemize}

\section{Power control}
\subsection{Sleep}
Any THz AP with no users is switched off ($p_a=0$), and users are re-associated. This
repeats for up to 3 rounds. A round is kept only if $S$ does not drop.

\subsection{Turn spare airtime into lower power}
AP $a$'s airtime load is
\[
L_a=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}_a}\tau_{u,a}\le1 .
\]
\why{$L_a$ is how much of AP $a$'s time is actually used. If $L_a<1$, the AP has spare time.}
If every user's time is stretched by $1/L_a$, the user still gets $\Rmin$ with a lower
capacity:
\begin{keybox}[Target SINR]
\[
c^\star_u=c_{u,a}\,L_a
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\gamma_u=\max\!\big(\gamma_{\mathrm{th}},\;2^{\,c^\star_u/B}-1\big)
\]
\end{keybox}
\why{Spare time can be traded for power. Give each user $1/L_a$ times more time, and it can live with a slower link $c^\star_u=c_{u,a}L_a$ and still get $\Rmin$. Inverting Shannon ($c=B\log_2(1+\gamma)$) gives the SINR needed for that speed; never below 5\,dB so the link stays up.}

\subsection{Yates minimum-power iteration}
\begin{keybox}[Power update]
\[
I_a(\mathbf{p})=\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}_a}
\frac{\gamma_u\Big(N_0+\sum_{b\ne a}p_b\,h_{b,u}\,G^{r}_{u,a,b}\Big)}{G_r\,h_{a,u}}
\]
\[
\mathbf{p}\;\leftarrow\;\min\!\big(\mathbf{I}(\mathbf{p}),\;\Pmax\big),\qquad \Pmax=20\ \text{dBm}
\]
\end{keybox}
\why{Each AP picks the smallest power that lets its \emph{weakest} user reach its target, given the interference it sees now. Changing one AP's power changes interference for the others, so we repeat until nothing changes. The $\min$ with $\Pmax$ keeps powers legal.}

\paragraph{Proposition 2.} $\mathbf{I}$ is a \emph{standard interference function}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Property & Holds because\\
\midrule
Positive & $N_0>0$\\
Monotone & all coupling terms are $\ge0$\\
Scalable: $\mu\,\mathbf{I}(\mathbf{p})>\mathbf{I}(\mu\mathbf{p})$ for $\mu>1$ & the $N_0$ term does not scale with $\mu$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At the current powers $\mathbf{p}^0$, every served user already meets its target, so
$\mathbf{I}(\mathbf{p}^0)\le\mathbf{p}^0$. Starting from there, the iteration only goes
\textbf{down} and converges to the minimum power vector $\mathbf{p}^\star$. At
$\mathbf{p}^\star$, every served user keeps $\Rmin$ and no AP exceeds its airtime budget:
\[
c_u\ge c^\star_u
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{\Rmin}{c_u}\le\frac{\tau_{u,a}}{L_a}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\sum_u\frac{\Rmin}{c_u}\le1 .
\]
\why{Every user's new speed is at least the planned $c^\star_u$, so its time need is at most the planned (stretched) share, and these shares add up to at most 1. Hence nobody loses service.}
\textbf{Result:} THz power drops from \textbf{1.40\,W to 0.53\,W ($-$62.1\,\%)}, with about 4 of
the 14 APs switched off. Satisfaction is unchanged.

\section{Escalation and search}
The stages run in order and stop at the first one that reaches $S\ge\eta=0.9$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l p{0.58\textwidth} l@{}}
\toprule
Stage & What it does & Physical cost\\
\midrule
\textbf{T0} & greedy GAP over THz + Wi-Fi at full power & none\\
\textbf{T1} & sleep + re-associate + minimum power & none\\
\textbf{T2} & genetic algorithm (GA) over azimuth $\phi$ and width $\alpha$ (1 budget unit) & none\\
\textbf{M} & GA over height, position within the room and beams (3 units), then Hungarian matching per room & movement\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
One budget unit is $40\times40=1600$ fitness evaluations. Final power control always runs at
the end.

\subsection{Fitness used by the search}
\begin{keybox}[Fitness]
\[
F=0.6\,S+0.4\,Q+0.5\cdot\mathbf{1}[S\ge\eta]-\beta M-\gamma C_p
\]
\end{keybox}
\why{$S$ is the main goal. $Q$ gives partial credit, so the search can tell \emph{almost} served from hopeless and climb towards better layouts. The bonus rewards crossing the 90\,\% target. $M$ punishes moving hardware, and $C_p$ stops APs from bunching up.}
\[
Q=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_u\min\!\Big(1,\;\frac{\max_a c_{u,a}}{\Rmin}\Big)
\qquad\text{(smooth link quality)}
\]
\[
M=\frac{\sum_a\big\lVert(x_a,y_a,z_a)-(x^0_a,y^0_a,z^0_a)\big\rVert}{|\mathcal{T}|\cdot\text{building diagonal}}
\]
\why{Total distance moved, divided by its largest possible value, so the penalty stays between 0 and 1.}
\[
C_p=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{i<j}\max\!\Big(0,\;1-\frac{d_{ij}}{d_{\min}}\Big),
\qquad d_{\min}=3\ \text{m},
\]
\why{Every pair of APs closer than 3\,m adds a penalty that grows as they get closer; averaged over all pairs.}
\noindent with $\beta=0.25$ and $\gamma=0.3$ (TeraMove configuration D).

\subsection{Hungarian matching}
New AP states are matched to physical APs inside each room so that total movement is
minimal:
\[
\sigma^\star=\arg\min_{\sigma}\sum_i C_{i,\sigma(i)},
\qquad
C_{ij}=\big\lVert\mathbf{r}_i-\mathbf{r}'_j\big\rVert .
\]
\why{All APs are identical, so it does not matter \emph{which} AP goes to a new spot. The Hungarian algorithm picks the pairing with the least total travel, in $O(n^3)$ time.}

\subsection{Commissioning}
The MILP layout's beams and heights were re-tuned once for the empty floor; the positions
did not change. Empty-floor coverage rose from \textbf{66.2\,\% to 100\,\%}.
